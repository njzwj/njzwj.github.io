---
title: "Keep things simple"
excerpt: "Set a clear goal, then remove unnecessary steps."
lang: en
---

During my first two years of work, I gradually realized that time and energy are limited. Wanting to finish a project is not enough to move complex work forward. As my responsibilities become more complicated, I need to define my goals more clearly and work with some uncertainty. Outside work, for example, I want to write, make vlogs, take photographs, learn languages, and exercise. Each activity takes sustained effort. When a project lasts months or years, efficiency matters greatly. Improving the efficiency of language learning by 20% could mean reaching a goal months earlier. Besides concentration and emotional control, one factor I can manage is the method itself: use simple, efficient methods, and avoid spending time on details that do not help the goal.

## Define the goal

The first step toward simplicity is to set a goal. It must be realistic and clear. A realistic goal is possible to achieve. A clear goal has specific indicators that show whether it has been achieved. These two qualities define both the scale and the direction of the work. Before starting, spend time examining the goal and learning enough about the subject to define it.

People often skip this step. Someone might want to learn an instrument or photography. But starting with the vague thought "I want to learn photography" does not provide specific actions to distribute across days or weeks and sustain over time. First understand why you want to take photographs. Then use that reason to define a clear goal.

For photography, I could ask myself:

- What kinds of photographs do I want to take? Do I have examples to study?
- What do I need to learn in the process?
- What do I want to achieve through photography? Do I want to create, express something, or keep a record?

After asking these questions and doing some research, I can use the answers to make a plan. The following actions might become clear:

1. Find and study photographs to develop my visual judgment.
2. Consider whether I need to buy a camera.
3. Set aside time each week to practice creating images or recording what I see.

A clear goal makes the work effective. It gives me a plan I can carry out.

## Accept uncertainty

As I learn more, my goals may change. I need to accept that uncertainty. Otherwise, it can make me hesitate, waste time, or turn the project into something increasingly complicated.

Starting is often better than spending too long deciding. The decision process itself must be efficient.

Some uncertainties can be resolved later as experience grows. Focusing too much on uncertainty assumes that I can choose the shortest path at the start. Usually, that path is not available to me.

## Keep things simple

The plan should be simple, practical, and directed toward the goal. This means remembering the goal whenever I make a decision.

Suppose I want to build a website with a chatbot for brainstorming and meeting notes. If another requirement appears during development, I should immediately check whether it is necessary. If it is not, I should leave it out.

A better-defined goal makes the final result more effective. Adding things halfway through does not necessarily do so.

Hideo Kojima is a well-known game creator. Over the past thirty years, he has created works such as Metal Gear. He closely controls production schedules and details such as gameplay, story, design, and music. That degree of control requires him to make decisions throughout the work.

Decisions are where goals meet practice. Efficient decisions serve his aims and ideas as a game creator. Those aims and ideas are the basis of success. The work that follows each decision should remain as simple as possible.

## Putting it into practice

Before starting a plan, and whenever a decision arises during the work, recall the goal and decide efficiently. If a choice remains uncertain, choose a reasonable compromise and continue.

Do not spend too much time on the decision itself.

This helps keep the work efficient.
